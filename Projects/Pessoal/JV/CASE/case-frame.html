1
Value Proposition & Pricing
Thesis
We don't sell the drug the way a pharmacy does. We sell a managed outcome program, which is both our differentiation and our defense on price.
Make the total cost transparent. The R$599/mo plan is the support layer; the medication is bought separately, so the price reads as an open-ended cost. State plainly what is included and show an honest all-in figure (service + drug) over the 6-month journey, quoted per week, so people stop mentally adding an unknown number and bouncing.
Defend the service premium with outcomes, not a discount. Voy sits on top of the drug, so the pitch is effectiveness and safety (its own claims: better results with medical and nutrition support), not "cheaper than a pharmacy", which is not a like-for-like we can stand behind.
Make it payable. Installments and an entry tier (lower starting dose, lighter service) remove the upfront barrier without discounting, which would erode margin and signal commodity.
Make the included care visible. The plan already bundles doctor, nutritionist and unlimited WhatsApp support; surface that so the customer sees a program, not a pill and perceives more for the same price instead of us cutting it.
Why it works
The "too high" (50%) and "confused" (30%) objections are linked: the R$599 shown may read as not including the drug, so people add an unknown cost and leave. Transparency on the all-in price plus an outcome-based reason for the service fee attacks both, without a discount claim we cannot defend. A pharmacy is a one-off transaction; Voy is a months-long managed journey.
How we measure
Aggregate CVR lift from an A/B test of the price changes on all traffic (the survey tells us what to test, the test tells us if it works), drop-off rate at the pricing / checkout step as the observable proxy for price friction and a continuous exit survey to watch the share citing price fall. Tier mix as a guardrail so the entry plan grows the base instead of cannibalizing the full plan.
2
Acquisition Strategy
Thesis
The bottleneck is conversion (0.5% CVR), not the channel or the CPC. We fix the funnel before buying more traffic.
Fix clarity on the landing (the 30%). Rebuild the page so the offer, eligibility and price are understood in seconds, since confusion is the second-largest reason people do not convert.
Proactive WhatsApp on hesitation. Voy already runs unlimited WhatsApp support and a free 1-day medical review; trigger the chat by dwell time on the price / eligibility step so a specialist reaches out at the moment of doubt. It serves all three objections at once: the human contact the 10% asked for, clarity on what R$599 includes for the 30% who are confused and the cost framing for the 50% who find it expensive. Lean team, two shifts on the evening browsing peak, run as an experiment with a holdout and read by cost per assisted conversion.
Diversify beyond Meta. Add high-intent Google Search (people already looking for GLP-1), partnerships and creators for trust and a referral program so customers bring customers. Meta is the primary channel today; these reach demand it does not.
Sharpen the targeting. Aim Meta at audiences who can realistically afford the plan (better lookalikes, interest and income signals), so we pay for clicks more likely to convert instead of clicks that bounce on price. Targeting handles "cannot afford"; the value proposition handles "can afford but found it expensive".
Why it works
CAC = CPC ÷ CVR and a $1 CPC is healthy, so doubling CVR is the cheapest way to scale. Search captures bottom-funnel intent that Meta prospecting does not and a broader mix reduces dependence on one channel while reaching demand Meta alone misses.
How we measure
CVR by funnel step to locate the drop-off, plus CAC, CPC and volume by channel (watching CPC as we sharpen targeting), cost per assisted conversion on the WhatsApp experiment (against a holdout) and referral CAC. Blended CAC is the north-star check across the mix.
3
High-Retaining Product
Thesis
In a GLP-1 subscription, retention is the business: CAC only pays back over months and the care wrap is the moat versus pharmacies. Most of that wrap already exists at Voy; the growth job is to deploy it proactively, not build from zero.
Make clinical adherence proactive. Voy already has doctors, nurses and pharmacists; the move is to turn support from reactive to scheduled, titration guidance, side-effect check-ins and reminders through the hard early weeks, when most would otherwise quit.
Turn coaching into a lifecycle. The nutritionist coaching already exists (rated 4.98); make it a proactive rhythm of check-ins, progress milestones and WhatsApp nudges, so a monthly refill becomes a relationship, not a transaction that is easy to drop. A dedicated app is optional, not required to start.
Continuity of supply. Guaranteed stock and auto-refill remove the top operational reason to leave, which is running out and buying elsewhere.
Maintenance plan against "graduation" churn. When patients hit their goal they tend to leave; a lighter maintenance tier keeps them and real weight-regain risk makes it a medical need, not just a retention trick.
Why it works
The likely churn drivers are side effects, cost, goal reached and cheaper at a pharmacy. Reliable supply plus care is a switching cost pharmacies cannot match and maintenance directly addresses the "goal reached" exit.
How we measure
Month-N retention curves, churn-reason mix from cancellation surveys, adherence rate andLTV/CAC as the payback guardrail that decides how hard we can scale.
4
Iteration Process
Thesis
Go-to-market is an experiment engine, not a one-off launch. The 20 non-converters are round one of a continuous loop.
Instrument the funnel first. Event tracking on every step so we see exactly where users drop before optimizing anything. You cannot improve what you do not measure.
Build a prioritized test backlog. Score ideas by impact and effort (ICE or RICE) against the three known objections, so the team works on what moves CVR most.
Run a weekly A/B cadence. A fixed rhythm of shipping and reading tests, so learning compounds instead of betting on one big redesign.
Feed learning back in. Winning tests become the new baseline, losing tests retire the hypothesis and the continuous exit survey keeps refreshing what to test next.
Why it works
A 20-person sample is directional, not conclusive, so the answer is not one perfect fix but a system that keeps finding and validating fixes. It also ties every recommendation to a measurable experiment rather than opinion.
How we measure
Tests shipped per week, win rate and cumulative CVR lift over time. Velocity of learning is the leading indicator; CVR is the lagging one.
5
Overall Category Strategy
Thesis
Beyond fixing the funnel, the biggest lever is growing the category itself: reach, clinical outcomes and trust. With the semaglutide patent gone and the market roughly doubling in 2026, the drug is commoditizing, so the service around it is what wins.
Expand the addressable audience. Move from obesity alone into adjacent needs (overweight, pre-diabetes, metabolic health) so the top of the funnel grows, not only its conversion.
Adherence and outcomes as the moat. In the real world about 40 to 50% of patients quit GLP-1 within 12 months, mostly on side effects and endocrinologist-led care cuts dropout by roughly 10%. Keeping the half that would drop and winning back those who return, is the largest growth pool and is copy-resistant because it rests on clinical protocol, not media spend. Supply is now hygiene (do not run out), not a moat.
Open B2B2C channels. Employers, health plans and clinics bring qualified members at a lower CAC than paid media and lend medical credibility.
Build medical authority and content. Doctor-led education grows the category and directly attacks the confusion objection, so it compounds with the acquisition work.
Why it works
The market is large and roughly doubling in 2026 (the largest pharmacy alone moves 150k units per month). With the patent expired the constraint is no longer supply but dropout and access: as the drug gets cheaper, demand expands and the winner is whoever keeps patients on therapy and earns their trust.
How we measure
Category share versus pharmacies, CAC and LTV in each new segment and channel, 12-month adherence and retention versus the market baseline and win-back rate of lapsed patients.
Sources: market outlook, InvestNews 2026. Real-world discontinuation (~40 to 50% at 12 months) and endocrinologist effect, Portal Afya. Return to treatment, Exame.

The growth ask · 2k to 4k with 50% better efficiency, this month
How I'd attack it this month and the path that makes 4k at $100 stick.
1 · The math
CVR 1% turns the same ~$400k and $1 CPC into 4k at $100. That is the target and every lever below drives to it.
2 · This month
Ship the fastest movers first: price transparency, landing clarity, WhatsApp assist and Meta creative and targeting tests. A 10 to 30% CVR lift in-month, with the experiment engine live from week one.
3 · Close the gap
The rest compounds across the phased plan, plus reallocating spend to higher-converting Search intent: same budget, better blended CVR.
4 · Cheapest scale
Lean into the lowest-CAC fronts in parallel: member-get-member and referral, plus reactivating the return cohort (most who stop GLP-1 come back). This is where the 50% efficiency gain compounds.
Operating health: LTV/CAC and month-1 / month-3 retention are the weekly gauge; they tell us where to accelerate and where to hold, so we scale on unit economics, not vanity volume.
Bottom line: fix conversion first, keep patients on therapy and the same budget delivers more customers at half the CAC, on a path we measure every week.

Growth Case · Q2 · Why efficiency plateaued
The budget is stuck in the saturating channel.
Meta · saturating
CVR 0.53% to 0.41%, CAC R$236 to R$462, ROAS 3.48 to 1.62. More budget, worse return.
Google · resilient
CAC R$152 to ~R$200, ROAS 6.1 to 4.4. Softening but still the best paid channel by far.
TikTok · fading
CVR 0.44% to 0.35%, ROAS 2.6 to 1.9. Following Meta down.
CRM and RAF · steady
ROAS near 20 every week, CAC ~R$47. Stable and starved of budget.
Attribution trap: under its own pixel Meta claims 102k acquisitions, but last-click gives it 92k, it over-credits itself. Google is the reverse (91k platform vs 95k last-click), under-credited. Optimizing to Meta's self-reported numbers over-invests in the saturating channel.
The plateau is a mix problem: 48% of budget sits in a saturating Meta while Google, CRM, RAF and Organic are underfunded and Programmatic burns R$3.9M below break-even. Blended CAC is dragged up by spending most where efficiency is lowest.
Growth Case · Q2 · Levers and reallocation
Same budget, better allocated
Top 3 efficiency levers
1. Cut Programmatic (ROAS 0.70, frees R$3.9M spent below break-even). 2. Fix or cut Affiliates (64% approval signals incentivized, low-quality traffic). 3. Shift Meta to the R$499 price point and top creatives, capping spend where marginal CAC exceeds target.
Top 3 scaling levers
1. Scale Google, the best mix of volume and efficiency (CAC R$193, ROAS 4.71). 2. Scale CRM and referral (RAF), ROAS ~20, the member-get-member engine from Q1. 3. Grow Organic (zero CAC) with content and SEO.
Reallocation at flat spend: cutting Programmatic and Affiliates frees ~R$4.9M. At Google's ~R$193 CAC that is ~25k acquisitions, versus the ~6k those two delivered. Net +~19k acquisitions, no extra budget, before even scaling CRM, RAF and Organic. The same money buys more because it moves to where CAC is lowest.
